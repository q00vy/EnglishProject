How to Remember English Words:
A Smart Guide for First-Year Students

Research Project by Matvey Stoilsky

Introduction

Hello! I am a first-year student, and like many of you, I study English. Every week we need to learn a lot of new vocabulary. But there is a big problem: we learn words for a test, and after two days, we forget them. Why does this happen, and how can we fix it?

The Problem: "Cramming" Doesn't Work

Many students use the "cramming" method (зубрежка). They read a list of words 50 times before the exam. This method is bad because our brain deletes information if we don't use it. This is called the "Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve". If you don't repeat the words, you will forget 70% of them in just one day!

My Research & Solution

I researched how human memory works and analyzed popular learning strategies. I found out that we need a system. Here are three simple and effective rules to remember words forever.

Rule 1: Context is King

Never learn words alone. Always learn them in phrases or sentences.

  • Bad: "Environment" - окружающая среда.
  • Good: "We must protect the environment from pollution."

When you have a context, your brain makes logical connections.

Rule 2: Spaced Repetition

You need to repeat words, but not every minute. You must repeat them with time intervals: after 1 day, after 3 days, after a week, and after a month. You can use free apps like Anki or Quizlet. These apps use smart algorithms to show you words right before you forget them.

Rule 3: Use the Words in Real Life

Your brain remembers information only if it is useful. Try to use new vocabulary in your hobbies. For example, if you like IT or programming, read articles about Python or SQL on GitHub in English. If you like music, translate the lyrics of your favorite rock or metal songs.

Conclusion

Learning English is not about having a perfect memory. It is about using the right strategy. Stop cramming, use spaced repetition, and put words into context. Study smarter, not harder!
