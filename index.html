Cramming vs. Spaced Repetition: How Students Can Remember English Words

Every week, university students memorize dozens of English words for vocabulary tests — and forget most of them two days later. This project analyzes why traditional cramming fails and tests a simple, research-based alternative.

MS
Matvey Stoilskiy

01 / Problem & Context

Why Do We Forget New Vocabulary So Fast?

First-year students at the University of Tyumen have to learn 30 to 50 new English words every week. Most students use "cramming" — reading a word list 30 times the night before class. It helps pass the test in the morning, but the words stay only in short-term memory.

Driving Research Question
How can first-year students remember English vocabulary longer without spending extra study time?
02 / Scientific Background

What Cognitive Science Says About Memory

Psychology research explains why cramming wastes time and why spacing repetitions across several days works much better.

~70%

Fast Memory Drop

Without repetition, the human brain deletes about 70% of new information within the first 24 hours.

Ebbinghaus; Murre & Dros (2015)
72% vs 90%

The Student Trap

72% of students choose cramming before tests, even though spaced repetition is more effective for 90% of learners.

Nate Kornell (2009)
8–12×

Context Meetings

To move a word from passive recognition into active speaking and writing, a learner needs 8–12 meetings in context.

Paul Nation (2013)

03 / Own Investigation

University Survey & 7-Day Mini-Experiment

To verify this problem at the University of Tyumen, I surveyed 20 first-year students in my group and conducted a 7-day vocabulary experiment with equal total study time (20 minutes).

Student Survey (N = 20)

How 1st-year ТюмГУ students prepare for English vocabulary tests:

Cram word lists the night before a test 80% (16/20)
Forget most words 1 week after the test 75% (15/20)
Use spaced repetition apps (Anki / Quizlet) 15% (3/20)

7-Day Experiment (15 Words Each)

Words remembered on Day 7 with the exact same total study time (20 minutes):

List A: Cramming (1 × 20 mins on Day 1) 27% (4/15)
List B: Spaced + Context (4 × 5 mins) 80% (12/15)

Result: Dividing 20 minutes into four 5-minute sessions tripled long-term retention (from 4 words to 12 words).

04 / Comparative Analysis

Comparing Three Vocabulary Methods

Summary of how different study strategies perform for first-year students:

Method How Students Study Day 7 Retention Best Use Case
1. Cramming Reading a bilingual word list 30–40 times in one evening. Low (~25–30%) Emergency pass for a quiz the next morning only.
2. Spaced Repetition Short reviews on Day 1, Day 2, Day 4, and Day 7. High (~75%) Building reliable long-term recognition quickly.
3. Context + Spacing Learning words in example sentences + spaced intervals. Best (~80%+) Active speaking, writing, and exam preparation.
05 / Practical Solution

3-Step Student Action Plan

A simple system any student can start using today without spending extra time:

Step 01

Write Short Context

Never memorize isolated words. Write one short sentence about your studies, hobbies, or daily life for each new word.

Step 02

Use 1–2–4–7 Intervals

Review new cards for 5 minutes on Day 1, Day 2, Day 4, and Day 7 using free apps like Anki or Quizlet.

Step 03

Connect to Interests

Read short posts or watch videos in English about topics you enjoy (IT, sports, music) to see words in real life.
